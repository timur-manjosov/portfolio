I'm Timur. I study mathematics, and I write code when reading about something
stops being enough. I rarely understand a method until I've built it myself
and made every possible mistake along the way.

## What I do

Mostly Discord bots, at the moment. [Aura](/en/projects/aura/) is the big
one: instead of archiving every message, it keeps a small model of what is
currently true on a server. When something changes, the new statement
replaces the old one. It comes with its own web interface.

[Epiphyte](/en/projects/epiphyte/) grows exactly one plant per server. The
plant lives on six activity signals and keeps a record of what happened to
it, scars from quiet stretches included.

[Saeculum](/en/projects/saeculum/) is a world simulator for the terminal.
Every event carries the causes that triggered it, so you can ask any war how
it started and follow the chain back. It has been resting since August.

For university I wrote [gauss-trainer](https://github.com/timur-manjosov/gauss-trainer),
a terminal program in Rust: you pick every row operation of Gaussian
elimination yourself, and it checks your work in exact fractions.

## Studies

I'm doing a mathematics degree by distance learning at FernUniversität in
Hagen, a German university built for remote study. My focus is cryptography,
with computer science as a minor. Cryptography, because it is where number
theory becomes very practical.

Some exercises I rewrite as programs. That is how gauss-trainer started: an
offline terminal version of a trainer from the course _Mathematische
Grundlagen_ (mathematical foundations).

In the longer run I'm heading towards quantitative work, the kind that deals
with risk, models and data.

## What I work with

Weighted the way it really is:

- **Python** is what almost all of my public projects are written in,
  including Aura, Epiphyte and Saeculum.
- **Java** is my most solid foundation, even though none of it is public.
- **Rust** I'm learning right now. gauss-trainer is the first bigger result,
  with about 195 tests.
- **C** I'm learning to see what happens underneath the higher-level
  languages.
- **Kotlin and Go** I know, but rarely use.

Day to day: Arch Linux on a ThinkPad, Neovim, the terminal and Git. How this
site is built is on the [Uses](/en/uses/) page.

## Where I stand

I like running my own things, because I want to know what happens underneath.
Anyone can host Aura themselves with Docker Compose. It has commands to see,
export and delete your own data, and its database can be encrypted. Epiphyte
gets only Discord's default permissions: it notices that someone wrote
something, never what.

This site follows the same rules: no cookies, no tracking, nothing loaded from
other servers. Its [source code](https://github.com/timur-manjosov/portfolio)
is public.

I'd rather learn one thing properly than ten things halfway, and I'd rather
check a convention than adopt it on trust.

## What else I'm into

Game theory, because it explains why people cooperate or don't, and because
its models so often disagree with what people actually do.

Systems where simple rules produce something nobody scripted. Saeculum and
Epiphyte both come from there.

And maths that makes pictures. The artwork on my
[GitHub profile](https://github.com/timur-manjosov/timur-manjosov) is a De Jong
attractor, the Rule 90 cellular automaton, a Koch curve and a set of Fourier
epicycles, all generated by one small Python script with no dependencies.
